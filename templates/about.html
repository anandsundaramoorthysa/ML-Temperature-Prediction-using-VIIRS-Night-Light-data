Project Methodology

  • Validated data quality (missing values, duplicates, data types)
  • Aggregated hourly/daily temperature data to monthly averages
  • Filtered to 2014-2024 period (VIIRS data availability)
  • Merged temperature and nightlight datasets on Year and Month
  • Final format: Year | Month | NightLight | Avg_Monthly_Temperature (132 monthly observations)

  • Visualized monthly temporal trends and seasonal patterns
  • Computed Pearson and Spearman correlations (Year, Month, NightLight vs Temperature)
  • Analyzed human activity-temperature associations at monthly resolution

  • Trained Multiple Linear Regression (3 features: Year, Month, NightLight), Random Forest, and Gradient Boosting models with regularization
  • Used Year, Month, and NightLight as features, Avg_Monthly_Temperature as target
  • Time-based split: 80% training (earlier months), 20% testing (recent months) to prevent data leakage

  • Evaluated models using RMSE, MAE, and R² metrics on testing set
  • Compared training vs testing performance to detect overfitting
  • Selected best-performing model based on metrics and overfitting control

  • Analyzed feature importance across models (Year, Month, NightLight)
  • Quantified human activity's (NightLight) contribution to monthly temperature prediction
  • Interpreted results in context of urban heat island effects and monthly patterns

Critical Limitations & Disclaimers

1. Causality vs. Association

This analysis demonstrates ASSOCIATION, NOT CAUSATION. While we observe relationships between urbanization indicators and temperature, correlation does not establish causal mechanisms.

2. Confounding Variables
  • Global climate change affects both urbanization and temperature
  • Natural climate variability not fully accounted for
  • Other local factors may contribute
3. Data Limitations
  • Moderate sample size (132 monthly observations, 2014-2024) - improved from 12 yearly but still moderate
  • Nightlight is a proxy, not direct urbanization measure
  • Monthly aggregation (vs hourly/daily) balances detail with data availability
  • Temporal restriction to 2014-2024 due to VIIRS sensor availability
4. Model Limitations
  • Models assume relationships can be captured by current features
  • Monthly resolution (132 obs) reduces but doesn't eliminate overfitting risk
  • Time-based validation helps but limited by dataset size
  • Limited ability to capture complex interactions
5. Generalization

Results specific to Chennai, India. May not generalize to other cities or climates. Regional factors specific to tropical/subtropical regions.
